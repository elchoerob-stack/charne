# Compliance checklists (South Africa)

These are working checklists, not legal advice. The agency's compliance
officer and the transferring attorney have the final word. When a rule below
matters to a decision, say so and suggest confirming with them. Last checked
November 2026.

## Property Practitioners Act 22 of 2019 (in force 1 Feb 2022)

- **Fidelity Fund Certificate (FFC).** No mandate, marketing or commission
  without a valid FFC for the current year, held by her and by the agency. A
  sale done without one can cost the commission. Check before quoting a fee.
- **Mandatory disclosure form (s 67).** The seller completes and signs it
  before the mandate is accepted. It goes to the buyer and is attached to the
  sale agreement. If a seller won't complete it, the mandate can't proceed.
- **Mandate.** It must state who is paid, how much (percentage or fixed fee,
  VAT treatment), the type (sole or open) and the end date.

## Signing

- **Offer to Purchase / deed of sale: wet ink only.** The ECT Act (s 4(4),
  Schedule 2) excludes agreements for the alienation of land from electronic
  signature. An e-signed OTP risks being void.
- Mandates, disclosure forms, FICA declarations and most other paperwork
  *can* be signed electronically (e.g. through DocuSeal).

## FICA (Financial Intelligence Centre Act 38 of 2001)

Estate agents are accountable institutions (Schedule 1, item 3).

- **Customer due diligence** for both seller and buyer, before the deal
  proceeds:
  - Individual: ID document and proof of residential address.
  - Company or trust: registration documents, the people acting for it, and
    the beneficial owners.
  - Higher-risk cases need more: source of funds/wealth, foreign or politically
    exposed persons. Follow the agency's Risk Management and Compliance
    Programme (RMCP).
- **No anonymous clients.** If identity can't be verified, the deal stops.
- **Cash over R49,999.99** (one payment or several linked ones) must be reported
  to the FIC on goAML as a cash threshold report, within the period the
  regulations set. Confirm the current deadline with the compliance officer.
- **Suspicious activity** (odd payment routes, rushed cash deals, a third party
  paying, reluctance to give documents) goes to the compliance officer for a
  suspicious transaction report. This has no amount threshold.
- **No tipping off.** Never tell a client a report was or will be made.

## POPIA (Protection of Personal Information Act 4 of 2013)

- Collect only what the purpose needs. Say why it's collected. Keep it no
  longer than needed.
- **Electronic direct marketing (s 69)** by WhatsApp, SMS or e-mail needs
  opt-in consent, unless the person is an existing client and the message is
  about similar property services. Every message must carry an opt-out, and an
  opt-out is honoured at once.
- **Lead lists bought or scraped** don't carry consent. Don't message them.
- **Sharing a buyer's details with a seller** (or the other way round) beyond
  what the transaction needs requires their agreement.
- Before sending any bulk message she drafts, check: did each recipient opt in,
  and is there an opt-out line?

## Compliance certificates on transfer (Tshwane)

- **Statutory:**
  - Electrical Certificate of Compliance, always.
  - Electric fence system CoC, if there is an electric fence.
  - Gas CoC, if there is a fixed gas installation.
- **Usually by contract, not by law, inland:** beetle (entomologist)
  certificate and plumbing certificate. Plumbing is a by-law requirement in some
  metros, such as Cape Town. The OTP decides. Check what the OTP and the
  attorney require.
- The seller normally pays for these.

## Before quoting numbers

- Transfer duty: run `scripts/costs.py duty`. Its table is dated. If the
  current date is after a February Budget, check the SARS page first.
- Bond rates move with the repo rate. Use the rate the bank has offered, or
  ask for one. Never assume prime.
- Attorney fees: get the attorney's quote. Never estimate them as fact.
